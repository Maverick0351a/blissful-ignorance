# Blissful Ignorance: implementation audit

October 6, 2026. Three specialized Codex subagents reviewed runtime/persistence,
learning/perception and the playable UI; the parent reviewed their evidence and
ran the complete existing suite. This is an internal software audit, not an
external certification or a new behavioral experiment.

**AUD-01, AUD-02 and AUD-03 are now fixed and installed locally.** Seven findings
remain open: six P2 functional defects/risks and one P3 initialization defect.
The original audit found ten issues and passed **218 tests in 98.123 seconds**;
the completed repair passed **231 tests in 95.756 seconds**, including
PettingZoo's compatibility checks. The original findings and reviewed source
locations below remain a record of the audit, with repair notes added.

The live service stayed private and manually paused at tick **84,935**, with
the same PID and eight PPO residents plus Laya's distinct NPU backend. The audit
used `/health` only for live checks, not `/api/state`, and did not read live
weights/journals, reset residents, promote policies or change production code.

## Completed preservation repair — October 6

GT-00 hardening, authorized after the audit:

- Playable startup and manual loading require a complete controller roster for
  already-controlled saves. Laya's saved identity cannot silently become a PPO
  controller. Scripted legacy migration retains its existing brains and backup;
  standalone experiments can still restore deliberately inactive bystanders.
  The population watcher and Laya arrival tool also request the complete-roster
  check. Malformed input files are left unchanged.
- Maximum-speed batches stop at the first controller pause. A failed decision
  restores every controller's weights, optimizer, predictor, RNG, hidden state,
  rollout and journal. A queued player action stays queued until a successful
  tick, and the error remains visible until a successful explicit retry.
- If rollback itself fails, or an exception occurs after world actions have
  begun, saving and advancing are blocked until a complete checkpoint is loaded
  or the app restarts. Partial physics/reward state is not claimed to be
  reversible; the last complete disk checkpoint is protected.

Thirteen new [fault-injection tests](../tests/test_preservation.py) cover missing
PPO/Laya controllers, a substituted Laya backend, rejected manual loads,
legacy migration, complete mixed saves, ordinary and update-boundary rollback,
an error after an actual Adam update, first/mid-batch failure, single execution
of a queued player action, failed restoration and partial world transitions.
The old missing-controller runtime test now checks a reported manual pause
instead of an uncaught exception. Initial failed checks are retained locally;
all final checks pass.

A separate paired synthetic eight-PPO run matched all **528 transitions** and
**38,275 final state values**, including one update per learner, against the
pre-fix source. No reward, architecture, policy choice or successful learning
update changed. Full decision snapshots cost time: this local PPO-only sample
took 11.715 seconds after versus 5.884 seconds before, about **45.1 ticks/second**
afterward. This is a bounded timing sample, not a live NPU throughput estimate.
Fast-forward throughput can be lower with preservation enabled.

The main server then stopped gracefully and its complete checkpoint was copied
to an ignored local backup. It restarted privately and manually paused at tick
**84,935**, with eight PPO residents and the existing Laya model, no controller
error and **86,727 identical saved values**. No life, model weight or private
history advanced during deployment. Laya's proposed retirement is a separate
pending selection; this repair did not replace her.

## Prioritized fix list

### AUD-01 · P1 · Incomplete saves can silently replace a resident's brain — fixed

**Where:** `agents/sequence_ppo.py:346`, `:279-286`;
`experiments/living_population.py:75`.

A readable all-controlled checkpoint with one missing brain and its metrics
passes subset validation. Startup fills that gap with a fresh PPO controller.
A synthetic Laya body with no saved Laya brain was accepted and became a fresh,
unrestored PPO brain. This is a malformed-save recovery defect; no such damage
was observed in the live population.

**Fix and acceptance:** Require exact body/brain identity equality for existing
all-controlled saves, including Laya's expected backend. Keep deliberate legacy
migration separate. Missing Laya or PPO entries must fail before writes; complete
mixed saves must restore normally. Preserve rejected files for recovery.

### AUD-02 · P1 · Maximum speed continues after a controller requests pause — fixed

**Where:** `server.py:215-218`; `experiments/living_population.py:89-95`.

The four-tick batch checks shutdown but not the pause set by a failed controller.
An isolated first-call failure was followed by three successful ticks: the world
advanced to tick 3 and cleared the controller error despite remaining paused.

**Fix and acceptance:** Break the batch immediately on pause/error and retain
the error until an explicit retry. Inject failures both at the first tick and
mid-batch; no further call may occur automatically after the failure.

### AUD-03 · P2 · A failed decision can manufacture learning experience — fixed

**Where:** `agents/sequence_ppo.py:296-305`.

Only external inference has rollback. When a later PPO decision fails, earlier
brains can already have changed their pending action, RNG, recurrent state or
optimizer while the world has not advanced. A fresh two-brain probe reproduced
an unexecuted move being recorded as a zero-outcome transition after retry.

**Fix and acceptance:** Make the complete decision phase transactional, or
require restoration of a known-good snapshot before retry/save. Restore full
brain state, not just counters. At a normal decision and an update boundary,
fault a later resident and compare every brain plus world state with an
untouched control. Include a Laya stub before the failing PPO. Treat loss of a
queued player action on the same failure path as part of this repair.

### AUD-04 · P2 · Experimental processes can share a checkpoint unsafely

**Where:** `experiments/living_population.py:165-186`.

The experimental serve/batch entry point does not acquire the ownership lease
used by the main server. Two invocations with the same directory can race on
the checkpoint and its shared temporary filename. The code path and a stub
construction probe confirm missing enforcement; concurrent file corruption
was not attempted or observed. The main server's entry point already leases.

**Fix and acceptance:** Lease the actual checkpoint directory before loading
and hold it through final save. A second serve or batch process must fail before
population creation/writes; separate directories must still work independently.

### AUD-05 · P2 · Paused construction obscures the placement area

**Where:** `web/world-first.css:12`; older building rule in `web/styles.css:2`.

Opening Build while paused combines an old `top:23%` rule with the new bottom
anchor. The browser reproduced a 367px-tall pause banner on a 502px map, covering
the traveler and neighboring tiles. It does not block clicks, but hides what
the player is placing.

**Fix and acceptance:** Override/remove the conflicting building top anchor.
Pause → Build must leave a content-sized banner and visible placement tiles
on desktop, at 390px, and after expanding/restoring the map.

### AUD-06 · P2 · Touch movement can leave the traveler off-screen

**Where:** `web/app.js:377,380`; keyboard counterpart at `:400`.

With the traveler already selected, pan away or use Everyone, then press a
direction button. The character moves but the camera remains in observer mode.
Keyboard movement separately enables follow. The discrepancy was reproduced
on the narrow layout.

**Fix and acceptance:** Centralize movement's camera reacquisition for keyboard
and touch. Test both inputs after Everyone/panning, with player and non-player
selected, while paused and while running. Each queued movement must execute once.

### AUD-07 · P2 · Browser shortcuts trigger game actions

**Where:** `web/app.js:400`.

The global key handler ignores Ctrl/Meta/Alt modifiers. Ctrl+F queued an eat
action in the browser fixture and consumed the next step. Other shortcuts that
contain game keys use the same path.

**Fix and acceptance:** Exclude modifier chords unless intentionally supported.
Ctrl/Meta+F, S and R must queue no gameplay action; plain F/S/R and Shift+? must
retain their intended behavior. Check canvas and navigation focus.

### AUD-08 · P2 · The action mask permits tapping during cooldown

**Where:** `agents/affordances.py:35`; `sim/social.py:3,76`.

A tap at tick 0 succeeds; at the next decision, tick 4, the mask still offers
tap, while the simulator rejects it under the eight-tick cooldown. This wastes
feasible-action choices across controllers. It does not establish why the
communication pilot failed.

**Fix and acceptance:** Expose the resident's own tap readiness and use it in
the mask. The mask must reject at tick 4 and allow at tick 8 with an eligible
neighbor, including after save/resume. This need not change neural dimensions
or reward formulas.

### AUD-09 · P2 · Read-only demo mode cannot start time

**Where:** `experiments/living_population.py:80`; public-demo control rejection
in `server.py`.

The learning runtime starts manually paused even in public-demo mode. A viewer
heartbeat cannot clear manual pause and the same mode forbids resume commands.
The isolated runtime probe remained paused after a view and rejected resume.
This is an existing local mode defect, separate from the documented unfinished
Hugging Face deployment work.

**Fix and acceptance:** Define an explicit operator-controlled autonomous start
policy for a separate demo population while preserving visitor read-only access.
Test that viewing the demo advances it, absence of viewers follows the chosen
pause policy, and visitor control requests remain rejected. Do not use the
private live population to test public exposure.

### AUD-10 · P3 · Initial action credit includes earlier waiting ticks

**Where:** `agents/sequence_ppo.py:97-104,272-278,318`.

Attaching a fresh brain at a preserved tick not divisible by four accumulates
reward before its first decision. A tick-1 start attributed three extra hunger
ticks to its first action: -0.005852 recorded versus -0.003348 for that action's
actual interval. This is a narrow attachment/migration edge case, not evidence
that the aligned practice experiments or ordinary resumed brains are invalid.

**Fix and acceptance:** Assign action credit only while an action is pending,
or clear unassigned credit at the first decision. Check tick remainders 0–3,
preserve whole-world metrics and reward formulas, and verify mid-rollout saves
retain their existing continuation.

## Lower-priority notes and limits

- Forced termination leaves a stale ownership file and blocks restart. This is
  already documented manual recovery. Improve precise launcher guidance or
  verified matching-owner cleanup; never remove a live owner's lock by age.
- The stale scripted-population paragraph in `docs/SENSES-AND-MEMORY.md` was
  corrected during this repair. It now distinguishes the historical baseline
  from the current PPO/Laya deployment and their different sensory inputs.
- Seed validation on raw `Brain.restore`, observation/action schema identifiers,
  and Laya artifact hashes are hardening suggestions. No affected production
  restore, current schema drift or changed external artifact was established.
- No category-head probability defect or Laya token overflow was found. The
  tokenizer/stub check fit within 512 tokens. This was not real NPU inference
  or an assessment of Laya's competence. Limited learned competence is not,
  by itself, an implementation defect.
